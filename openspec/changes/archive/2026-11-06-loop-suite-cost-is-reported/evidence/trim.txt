## What a fixture costs — the premise the merge rests on

`new_project` (`tools/loop-cases.sh:804`) is the prepared project every one of those cases builds: six `mkdir`
targets, two `cp`s (`tools/tooling.json`, `tools/pi-child-events.sh`), and `git init`, two `config`s, `add`,
`commit`. Probe — that same sequence five times, timed with `date +%s%3N`:

```

2026-11-06T10:24:03Z
```

13 ms of a 1.10–1.64 s case, i.e. **0.8–1.2 % of it**. The rest is the case's own assertions.

## The arithmetic the merge was priced with

All 74 cases merged into as few as one per fixture removes one setup per case merged:

- worst case (73 setups removed): 73 × 0.013 s = **0.95 s of the 101.97 s** those three groups pay — **0.9 %** of
  them, and nothing at all of the suite's other cases.

## What was done with it

No case was merged. The trim's own justification — "each builds a prepared project per case", so the cost is setup —
is 0.8–1.2 % of a case here, and a merge that touches 74 case bodies to save 0.9 % of 101.97 s is a change of its own
size and its own risk for a number the report now makes readable. `merged-cases.txt` records the empty mapping; no
mutation record named a merged case, so none needed re-pointing.

A trim aimed at a group whose cases really do pay is a change of its own, and the report is what lets that change aim
at the group that pays rather than at the one that looked expensive.
